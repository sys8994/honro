# Stage 6 rock material study (not integrated)

This is an isolated material study, not a stage BODY or a gameplay-ready asset. `rock-study.png` is a generated modular rock concept based on the dusk-green palette and material language of the earlier Stage 6 concept; its silhouette is not the game's collision and must not be scaled into place as a floor. The prototype's physical source is `tools/stage6-prototype/author.mjs`, SHA-256 `1c3f1f1825e39cabf653688ed779dcc42d80ce448e4e582173a8eb53cbb80870` at the time of this study. It remains unapproved geography.

`rock-study-raw.svg` is the Inkscape 1.4 trace, with the original reference preserved separately as PNG. Trace Bitmap settings: Multicolor / Colors, 8 scans, smooth and stack off, speckles 2, smooth corners 1.00, optimize 0.200. The near-white background trace and embedded reference were removed. It is a raw trace, not an optimized runtime asset. It has seven large color paths and substantial fine texture. An Inkscape Simplify 0.002 experiment produced holes and was rejected, not committed as a candidate. The render was reviewed at 1086×362 against the source; the broad rock bands remain, but high-frequency texture and unsupported slope prevent acceptance. No source-to-runtime JSON, collision alignment, bridge components, gameplay, mobile or final review is claimed.

Next: make separated source-traced rock/bridge components with controlled texture, fit to the actual stabilized physical contour without adding visible support surfaces, perform bounded optimization preserving contact edges, compile through `tools/vector-production/import-svg.py`, and review actual gameplay cameras and input play. The old BODY is locked to different geometry and must not be silently reprojected.

## Cleaner module experiment

`rock-module.png` is a separate generated solid-color rock concept. Inkscape 1.4 Trace Bitmap used Multicolor / Colors, eight scans, Stack on, smooth off, speckles 2, smooth corners 1.00 and optimize 0.200. The near-white background and embedded raster reference were removed from `rock-module-raw.svg`; the PNG is retained separately. It is an independent rock component, not a collision surface or a finished map. Raw SHA-256: `175ffe0fdb49bc57aa5300e153333a181cabcbe494a89f644a2d24f7f70aad3a`.

A non-cumulative Inkscape simplification search at 1086×362 and 2172×724 selected 0.0003. The raw trace contained 1181 commands; the candidate contains 756 and 31 subpaths, 39,337 SVG bytes. At 1× RGB MAE was 0.126/255 and maximum silhouette offset 1 px; at 2× RGB MAE was 0.103/255 and maximum silhouette offset 1 px. There were no contact-edge protections in this independent unplaced material study, so these numbers do not approve an eventual ground contact. The 0.001 candidate failed the silhouette limit. I inspected the raw/optimized comparison and standalone actual vector renderer; large facets and the outer form remain readable. `rock-module-runtime.json` is generated with the adjacent explicit config through `tools/vector-production/import-svg.py`; the normal vector/environment validators returned zero errors. The foot in that config is placeholder metadata, not a verified support anchor. Placement, source-to-contour fit, bridge work, runtime gameplay cameras, mobile, and final review remain open.
