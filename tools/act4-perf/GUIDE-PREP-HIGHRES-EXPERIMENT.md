## Vector identity and guide preparation

In a fixed-camera stage-40 cold water investigation, the actual battle landmarks for the 13 approved terrain/structure assets each retained the same vector object across their draws; already-prepared vectors did not construct new Path2D objects. Those landmark vector objects were distinct from their same-ID provider library objects. The separately selected ferry vector was the provider object. A prewarm against library objects alone would not warm the battle landmark WeakMap. On entering the water view, `terrain-right-join` created 51 Path2D objects and each newly encountered pile/ramp/timber extraction created 26. The original 233 total includes clip paths and other objects. This is one instrumented Chromium sample; it does not cover all stages, mutation cases or cache lifetimes.

Separately invoking actual `HonroVectorArt.validate` on the battle vectors took 9–16ms for the relevant extraction assets, about 50ms for the right join, and 185ms for already-prepared BODY. `prepare` of not-yet-prepared extraction vectors took 12–20ms, right join 64.5ms; that call includes its own validation. Already-prepared BODY returned immediately. Timings are one headless sample with host/JIT effects, not additive nor mobile estimates. The current synchronous validation traverses a complete vector and individual `Path2D` constructors can be large: scheduling one whole asset in requestIdleCallback does not enforce a small task and can move a hitch into guide/input time. No guide preparation is adopted. A viable candidate must validate the exact battle object without missing mutation, yield during validation/preparation, handle cancellation and cache atomicity, and measure both guide/input delays and cold entry. Simply warming provider objects or silently trusting object identity is insufficient.

## Higher-resolution bucket experiment — not adopted

This is a separate idea from the rejected screen-cache settling experiment: for the opted-in Act 4 terrain tiles, use half-octave instead of quarter-octave scale buckets, choosing a scale no lower than the original after the existing tile limit. Actual paths, tiles, clipping, camera, and artwork remain unchanged; rasterizing at a higher scale and resampling can alter edge AA. The isolated QA build used stage 38/40 fixed 1440×900 DPR1 scenes, the App loop stopped, three 16-frame .94→.60→.94 zoom cycles, then 12 water frames with changing scene time. Candidate and baseline were each run once (baseline 38→40, candidate 40→38). Times are CPU-side render durations, not normal UI input, FPS or a mobile-device result; no contemporaneous CPU/RAM/process census was recorded. Small samples and a shared host preclude a performance pass.

In the three zoom cycles, 38 baseline built 48 tiles per cycle and candidate 30/27/27; p95/max 114.9/114.9/115.9 versus 113.9/101.1/117.4ms. 40 baseline built 44 per cycle and candidate 27; p95/max 118.4/115.5/119.0 versus 125.4/133.4/128.8ms. First/maximum water draw 38 was 196.6→220.0ms, 40 was 490.6→578.6ms. Fewer tile creations did not produce a reliable p95 improvement and the candidate was not adopted for this performance reason, not because any difference in pixel hash is automatically forbidden.

At the changed zoom frame 3, 38 had 130,095 differing pixels, maximum per-channel difference 57 and 9,810 pixels over 10; 40 had 98,508 differing pixels, maximum 97 and 9,710 over 10. Direct side-by-side inspection of the whole view and a 480×300 crop showed the same visible structures and character readability, and the amplified difference was concentrated on edges; this is not proof that all differences are AA or that no tile seams exist. A simple Sobel-threshold edge-distance probe gave 99th-percentile distances ≤1.42 pixels for 38 and ≤1 pixel for 40 but maxima of 48/95 pixels due to unmatched threshold edges; this does **not** determine maximum contour displacement. Missing/extra features, quantization or seams outside the shown views, precise maximum geometry displacement, real normal input, mobile DPR and UI remain unverified. No visual acceptance is claimed. Fixed frames on the other equal-scale samples matched exactly.
