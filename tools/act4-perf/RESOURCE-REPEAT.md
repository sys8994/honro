Each sample used a fresh one-page headless Chromium context, warmed with four renders before the fixed idle/pan/zoom/stepped/water sequence. Execution order was 38 baseline/candidate, twice, then 40 baseline/candidate, twice. Three pre-existing Chromium roots remained active between samples; thus this is a lower-load comparison, **not** a completely isolated host. Available RAM 6.3–7.0 GiB, host load roughly 6–8 during samples; exact timestamps, process counts, RAM, load, all frame times and hashes are in `evidence/controlled-resource.tar.xz`, SHA-256 `bd0737769993053a4ff32693cf5bb2ac6410942e368d1009f846cf9acb79e09b`. Neither source revision nor camera/asset/fixture changed between repetitions. The candidate includes the Path2D rig cache and independent prewarm scheduling, not the discarded raster experiment. Timing is CPU-side Scene.render duration, not rAF frame interval or real-phone FPS.

In the 18-frame idle sample, stage 38 Path2D allocations fell 54→0 in both pairs, with median 2.0–2.1 ms before and 2.1–2.3 ms after. Stage 40 fell 108→0 with median 2.9–3.2→2.6 ms. In the 12 zoom samples, medians were 51.5–52.1→53.5–58.5 ms on 38 and 46.1–52.0→44.1–44.6 ms on 40. Maximum zoom render durations remain much longer; the 40 initial water-location draw also remains a large cold/tile hitch. The stage 38 pan became slower in these two samples. Final hashes match across each fixed scenario, but this scoped test does not establish gameplay, all frames, p95 acceptance or phone performance. This identifies a reproducible allocation reduction, not a complete hitch fix.
