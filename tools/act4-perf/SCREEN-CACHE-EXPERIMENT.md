# Screen raster settling experiment (not adopted)

Fixed-input investigation only; base RC `0282ac196`, branch rig change `180282845`, unchanged RC stage 38/40 candidate bytes. Headless Chromium, 1440×900 DPR 1, controlled real `Scene.render`, not a normal playthrough or phone FPS. The test changed raster allocation from the second identical view invocation to the fourth; image resolution, source art and tile geometry were unchanged. It also included the rig cache, so timings are not an isolated measurement of raster policy alone. Raw timings, cache counters, frame hashes and legacy 1/11 browser checks are in `evidence/screen-cache-experiment.tar.xz` (SHA-256 `d898d49eda9ba6c24c1263c00148170ad5fa8501d348028c19c099177a24d807`).

The two-draw-per-position stage 38 path built 42 screen rasters before versus 0 during motion in the experiment; a later settled view built three. Its observed median draw was 33.7 ms versus 4.8 ms on a repeated run, while zoom and initial water/tile work still caused long stalls. Stage 40's corresponding screen builds fell 28 to 0, with unchanged 15 domain tile builds. Real UI pan results were noisy: p95 was 166.7→183.3 ms in one sample and 166.8→133.3 ms in another, despite screen builds falling 45→6. This does not establish an overall p95 pass.

Crucially, per-frame hashes on stage 38 differed in 13/28 stepped-pan and 2/8 water frames; idle, pan, zoom and settled hashes matched for every sampled frame. Final frame hashes in the stage 38 and 40 scenarios matched. The existing renderer's direct versus screen-cached paths are not universally pixel identical: on legacy stage 1 and 11 mobile DPR 2, the unmodified baseline already differs by respectively 128 and 155 pixels, maximum channel difference 3. The same differences occurred in the experiment. The experimental screen policy is therefore **not adopted**: a final-frame match cannot establish unchanged animation frames. Stage 40 per-frame comparison and phone performance remain unverified. Further work must isolate direct/cache antialiasing and tile/zoom cost without reducing the artwork or silently tolerating new differences.
