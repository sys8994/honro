# Stage 39 instrumented finite probes

The predeclared policies and pinned inputs are in `tools/qa-stage39-observer/plan*.json`. Every run starts from the same declared 38-cleared level-25 fixture and game-created seed 19388; it does not represent actual completion of prior chapters. `progress-first` prioritizes safe support movement before local predicted attacks, while `threat-first` first considers a local eligible attack. Both use the actual App and keyboard controls; the predictor is used only for attack choice. Browser processes in each pair ran concurrently, so their elapsed times are not a performance comparison. No enemy or resident was removed or altered to meet a metric.

The two first v1 runs ended won in rounds 4 and 6, both with 27 foes alive. Resident damage events of 59 and 63 HP respectively were recorded. The v2 repeats, with impact observation and aggregated movement, ended won in rounds 5 and 6, with respectively 27 and 26 foes alive; one v2 resident took 106 HP damage. These are all retained, not selected by outcome. Differences under a common seed do not establish a causal effect of strategy: input holds and browser frames run in real time, and instrumentation and concurrent load can affect timing. No live battle state or RNG was changed by the observer.

The engine `wait()` hook did not observe normal UI defense: `App.defend()` is a separate path. Consequently an empty v1/v2 wait list is not a zero-defense result. A v3 pair with the same policies adds observation of that actual UI method; its test plan was committed before those runs. No method-call count alone proves intent, a visible receipt, successful evasion or a human win rate.

Each compressed trace is the raw observer method record; `events.jsonl.xz` contains the controller actions and periodic support/mission state. `result.json` preserves the original result and hashes. The second pair includes a real warning-frame screenshot and `summary.json`; a warning being recorded does not by itself verify its reading duration or avoidance. A `unit-impact` is an actual `Engine.impact` unit collision, not necessarily HP loss; a `hurt` HP/shield change is a separate event. Damage attribution is limited to the engine's supplied owner and skill; missing values are unknown.

The v3 pair adds `App.defend()` and planner aggregation and ended won in rounds 5 and 6 with 27 and 26 foes respectively. Recorded unit impacts were 31 and 36, and observed UI defense calls with state changes were 12 and 13, plus 4 and 3 retreat-end calls. A `unit-impact` can hit an ally or enemy and is not necessarily damage. The first v3 run recorded one resident HP loss of 100 from the wreck; the second recorded resident losses of 62 and 59 from separate enemies. There were 0 dropped observer rows. Its round/phase fields are post-call, so a call that changes turns can be attributed to the wrong boundary. v5 records both entry and exit context, with a fixed plan committed before execution. Prior results remain unchanged.

The v4 repeat records Playwright keyboard calls, mouse clicks/wheels and explicit timeout requests through terminal state. It does not record mouse movement (the runner moves to the viewport center before the tide camera wheel), the actual wall duration of condition waits or screenshot/renderer scheduling, and is not a complete physical-input timing trace. v5 has the same omission. Both runs won, in rounds 4 and 6; their command-stream SHA-256 values are in their result files and verified against the included `ui-inputs.json`. Its observer is v3, so the method-entry phase limitation above still applies.

The v5 pair records entry and exit context around observed calls. Both completed (5 and 6 rounds); each completed enemy phase had six distinct enemy IDs calling `finishAction`, but these are not raw call counts: the engine invokes it for both transition to review and review completion, and an additional repeated call occurred. Whether this matches an external 5–7-action rubric depends on that rubric's definition. There were zero dropped rows. Warning screenshots each show the actual pilot warning; following controller snapshots show an allied response turn, but warning exposure duration and causal evasion success were not proven. The v5 input trace omitted pointer movement and is retained with that limitation.
