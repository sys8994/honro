The 7 and 27 are `hurt` calls whose enemy owner and skill were recorded, source was normal, and target was a hero or resident; several calls may belong to one attack, and events are correlated. The loss percentages divide **each event** by the target hero's known maximum HP. They do not estimate chance to hit, damage per attack, damage as a percentage of intended power, or probability of survival. Shield-only losses are real pressure even when HP-only loss is zero. Progress-first also had one resident loss of 59 HP from `a4-39-n-3`/`HMO01`; that event is included in the 7 and point totals but omitted from the percentage distribution because this trace did not capture the resident's maximum HP. Threat-first had no resident HP-loss call in this run.

The other 16 / 27 calls include hero-to-enemy loss (9 / 18), source-unknown loss (4 / 4), self-loss (2 / 3), and enemy-to-other-enemy loss (1 / 2). These categories are disjoint in this particular enumeration; the JSON also exposes overlapping flags. None was recorded with an explicit non-normal source. The four unknown-source calls per run must not be relabeled environmental attacks. The captured water-recovery arrays were empty; that does not establish why the unknown losses occurred. Actual App defense observations separately show 6 / 10 positive HP recovery events totaling 284 / 496 HP; healing is not subtracted from, or attributed to, hostile hits.

The two additional direct-collision IDs in progress-first are `a4-39-n-4` (knight) and `a4-39-w-5` (occultist). Their actual unit impacts are evidence of contact, not proof of intended targeting, damage or the player's awareness. All party/resident impact owners and target IDs are in the JSON. Enemy fire calls numbered 11 / 26, but the fire hook does not record intended target: missed or off-screen shots cannot be counted as party pressure on that basis. Enemy terrain-impact calls were 2 / 0; the trace does not link either to a player choosing a different route or attack. No additional occlusion-driven decision is therefore claimed. The unconfirmed 17 / 15 enemies are not proven inactive, unreachable or harmless.

For action counts, the fixed key is `(run, entry round, enemy ID)` and requires an enemy-side `finishAction(reviewed=false)`; all repeat calls for that tuple and `reviewed=true` review completions are merged. The completed enemy rounds contain six unique actions each: rounds 1–3 in progress-first, rounds 1–5 in threat-first. The raw corresponding method calls are 12, 12, 13 and 12, 12, 12, 12, 12 respectively; duplicates are not extra actions. This supports the 5–7 target **only if** it refers to that per-phase unique-actor definition. A finish call alone is not effective pressure.

The actual pilot warning is visible in both saved PNGs. In progress-first its marked knight had a later turn and defended before the recorded shot; this is a response opportunity and action, not proof the player perceived the warning or successfully evaded. In threat-first the marked knight did not act before victory, although other party turns occurred; this run does not prove the marked target could respond in time. Both mechanisms and the residents' safe-return gate completed, but the logs do not establish a deliberate, effective escort-defense choice. No automatic pass is assigned to the warning-evasion or escort-choice criteria.

The stated design target is an **18–20-turn median**; these two fixture-driven outcomes have a median of 5 turns, 13–15 turns below that target. The observed 10 / 12 direct-evidence unions are below 18, but are conservative **unique-enemy** observations, not a validated definition of the 18–26 effective-engagement metric, so that criterion remains unmeasured rather than failed by arithmetic. The separately recorded screen-led 11-turn saved-branch run used a different policy and previously learned map; it is evidence that the longer visible route encountered and attacked threats, not a blind-player median.

**Design interpretation:** the traces demonstrate that an intact 27-enemy encounter can finish early after normal rescue interactions and that some enemies deliver meaningful collisions, HP loss and shield loss. The short observed routes leave many enemies without confirmed contact before exit, and enemy turns activate only six distinct actors per completed phase. Together these are evidence of a possible exposure/pacing gap relative to the intended 18–20 turns, not proof that most AI is broken or that skill alone explains the difference. The short routes were designed with prior map knowledge, and the logs cannot distinguish unobserved target attempts from route/height/line-of-sight limitations. Before changing balance, define the effective-engagement unit and record target-resolved intent, reaction timing and representative player route/escort decisions. If those measurements confirm the gap, review whether existing enemies' coverage or warning/patrol placement intersects the optional rescue route and whether a readable, avoidable escort threat provides a genuine choice. Preserve the existing terrain, exit condition, four residents, optional wreck, enemy count and HP while evaluating such hypotheses; no runtime, encounter data or difficulty has been changed by this analysis.
