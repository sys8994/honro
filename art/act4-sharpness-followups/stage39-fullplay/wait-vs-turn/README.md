# Stage 39: waiting versus turn progression

This is a diagnostic of one real saved battle, not a normal full playthrough, an exhaustive bypass test, or a balance approval. Headless Chromium 1440×900 loaded the same game-produced round-10 storage in two sequential independent contexts. The original is `../screen-route-eleven-round/round-10-storage.json.xz` (decompressed SHA-256 `f71125142de98506d13c823038a74419abfa155579d90544681e2c645384bd32`). Net release and route stabilization were already achieved through the original UI. The preceding journey used a declared 38-cleared entry fixture, not actual play of all earlier chapters. The real stage-39 candidate SHA is `0bb8dd43bf33af941c88800c4e7e13bbf2c7d3dd924444c035c997daad8f5dcb`; the loaded build manifest and observer hashes are in `result.json`.

The candidate is locally imported into the actual App, Story, Engine and Renderer as a QA fixture. Both contexts used the actual Continue button. The idle context received no further input; the other used 12 normal F inputs, with no player attacks. The instrumentation records calls and snapshots without injecting position, HP, phase, RNG, receipts or success. Times are wall time since observer installation after Continue, sampled approximately once per second. Resident movement between save restoration and the first sample is material and included below. Screenshots, compressed timeline and trace, and hashes are alongside this note. Page errors and observer failures were zero in both contexts.

In the idle branch all 61 samples were visible, with no dialogue, modal or story turn pause. The game clock advanced approximately 60.56 seconds. At the first sample the four had already moved about 113–115 units since the original save, leaving 2.35, 0.002, nearly 0 and nearly 0 of the respective remaining budgets; by the last sample all budgets were effectively zero. The observer was installed after resumption, so its trace alone does not include every movement between the saved snapshot and attachment. In the turn branch the first enemy phase was visible at about 8.7 s, the first new artillery history entry at 20.9 s, three safety flags at 23.9 s, the second new artillery entry at 47.5 s, and the HP reduction by 48.5 s. These are sampled observation times, not precise impact times or damage attribution. One turn-branch sample had a pause/dialogue/modal/hidden condition; the complete flags are retained in the timeline.

The code provides an important qualification: Stage 39 `escort` requires both gates, moves only during side 0/aim, and issues each resident a 170-unit budget when the `round:shot:side` token changes. Thus real time alone can spend the *remaining* budget with no enemy response, as observed here, while a successful shot also changes the token: this is not simply one budget per complete round. This saved state did **not** demonstrate all residents reaching safety through waiting alone; other positions or renewed shot tokens may behave differently and were not tested here. Whether a resident can reach safety within a remaining budget without a meaningful threat opportunity, and whether that explains the earlier 4–6-round runs, remain open. These results do not establish a balance defect or justify changing counts, rules or Stage 40 free escort. Damage events and total multi-hit attack damage must also not be conflated.

Reproduction: `python tools/qa-stage39-wait-probe/probe.py` in the QA build environment, with the named original saved storage, candidate and far asset at the local paths in the script. The output directory must not already exist. The test captures actual browser frames but does not establish physical-device behavior or a complete mission.
