## Separate production-Engine diagnosis, not gameplay

The first coarse mage entrance grid tested starts x=360..445, direction holds up to .85 **simulation seconds**; its 2/120 result understated longer holds. A follow-up cloned the same fixed candidate and entry battle at rest with slope-derived synthetic positions x={425,430,435,440,445,450}, jump delays {0,.05}, and right holds {.75,.85,.95,1.05}, integrating at 1/120 s. All six starts and both delays reached the middle tread at both .95 and 1.05 s; at x440/445/450, .85 s also succeeded. There were 30/48 successful sampled combinations, not a probability or a complete continuous boundary. This and the actual (436,2270) UI success show that the first mage entrance failure cannot by itself establish a pixel-narrow or impassable entrance: maintaining direction is material.

For the west link's y=1282.5 tread, synthetic x={230,245,260}, jump delays {0,.05,.1}, left holds {.03,.06,.09,.12,.18} produced 19/45 sampled grounded upper landings for mage and 15/45 for knight. At x230 all sampled successful jumps had zero delay; at x260 some with .05 s delay succeeded. The displayed 45-world-unit tread width and 81–99-unit successive rises are real geometry; the grid is sparse and does not measure actual human input timing or a continuous safety interval. The UI failure at x236 has a concrete start-edge explanation, but repeated falls across multiple heroes keep usability unresolved.

At the east y=2997 approach, the earlier synthetic archer sweep tested starts x={6520,6535,6550,6565,6580}, delays {0,.05,.1}, and holds {.25,.30,.35,.40,.45,.50}. ## Decision and smallest follow-up

Do not change collision solely on the evidence so far: east failures include a start 3.5 units from an edge, an airborne re-jump and a multi-step overshoot, and the scoped sweep plus normal 7-turn lower completion show viable approaches. Likewise, the west-entry failure is explained by maintaining direction and all four actually entered; the refined grid covers more than one sampled launch point. The 45-unit west-link treads remain a **credible input-usability concern**, not a proven impossible path and not yet a human-friendly pass. The lower path provides a separately verified alternative, subject to actual tide timing, so this does not justify changing a mission predicate or lowering enemies.

If stable-frame UI tests or representative players reproduce repeated west-link edge refusals after jumping before direction and waiting for landing, propose a **local, QA-only** candidate at `approved33-west-link` near the tested x≈225–270/y≈1282.5 join: widen the visibly supported stone tread modestly at its approach edge and adjust only the matching natural stone silhouette so the foot remains visibly grounded. Keep its height, neighboring cliff/arch, water, story coordinates, foes and other routes unchanged; check overlapping higher surfaces and test all four characters before selecting a width. This is a design hypothesis, not an approved collider coordinate or an implemented fix. If the art already gives sufficient physical support and input-order tests are comfortable, prefer retaining collision and providing a small in-world route cue that distinguishes the western climb from the lower tide path. Either option requires actual Chromium movement, same-camera art/footing review and a fresh full run; no hidden timing window or synthetic success should be treated as acceptance.
